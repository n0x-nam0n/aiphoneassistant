Below is the pre-build product spec. The first paid version is deliberately centered on overflow capture for private dining, catering, large-party, and missed-call inquiries so we can prove recovered revenue before expanding the product surface.
AI Restaurant Revenue Desk
MVP Product Specification
Working product concept: An overflow-first AI phone capture layer for independent restaurants that answers calls staff could not take, detects high-value private dining/catering/large-party inquiries, qualifies those leads, and sends structured opportunities into the restaurant’s existing workflow.
Working positioning:
Never lose another high-value private dining or catering inquiry because your team was too busy to answer the phone.

The dedicated events line is the personalized sales demo. Overflow is the default paid deployment because it intercepts calls that already exist and makes recovered-revenue claims defensible.


1. Product Goal
Build a lightweight restaurant-specific AI receptionist that prioritizes revenue-generating inquiries rather than general call automation.
The initial product should help restaurants:
- capture missed calls
- answer common questions
- identify private dining inquiries
- identify catering inquiries
- identify large-party inquiries
- collect structured lead information
- send instant confirmation to the caller
- alert restaurant staff
- organize leads in a simple pipeline
- track estimated opportunity value
- preserve transcripts and call summaries
The product should not initially attempt to replace the restaurant's full phone operation or event CRM.
Its first job is to prevent valuable inquiries from becoming voicemail or abandoned calls, then hand the structured lead to the restaurant’s existing system when one exists.

3. Core Customer Problems
Problem 1: Calls arrive when staff cannot answer
During busy service periods, front-of-house employees prioritize guests standing in front of them.
Incoming callers may:
- hang up
- leave voicemail
- call a competing restaurant
- forget to follow up
A $5,000 event opportunity can therefore be lost because nobody answered a 90-second phone call.

Problem 2: Private dining inquiries are poorly structured
Restaurants often receive leads such as:
"Hi, I'm looking for somewhere for a company dinner next month."

The manager then has to manually determine:
- date
- party size
- event type
- budget
- room requirements
- food format
- beverage requirements
- accessibility requirements
- contact information
- preferred follow-up method
This creates repeated phone/email back-and-forth.

Problem 3: Leads disappear into inboxes and voicemail
Many independent restaurants do not have a proper event-sales CRM.
Potential bookings may live in:
- voicemail
- Gmail
- text messages
- handwritten notes
- contact forms
- manager memory
There is no clear pipeline or attribution.

Problem 4: Existing AI receptionist platforms are broader than necessary
Many competitors emphasize:
- reservations
- ordering
- POS integration
- payment
- every incoming phone call
That creates more complexity and integration requirements.
Our initial wedge is:
High-value restaurant lead capture.


4. Core Value Proposition
For restaurant owners:
Capture private events, catering requests, and large-party leads even when nobody can answer the phone.

For managers:
Stop listening to voicemails and manually qualifying every inquiry.

For event coordinators:
Receive structured, ready-to-follow-up opportunities instead of incomplete phone messages.

For restaurant groups:
See event demand across locations in one dashboard.

Overflow deployment mechanics
The v1 paid deployment uses conditional carrier forwarding: the restaurant enables unanswered/busy forwarding from its existing advertised number to the AI number. Store that customer-facing number as `forwarded_main_number`. Store the separate manager/secondary-office destination as `human_transfer_number`. Do not assume one carrier code works everywhere; setup is carrier- and device-specific.

Required operations workstream:
- maintain a per-carrier setup and rollback sheet
- verify forwarding with a live test call before activation
- record the restaurant's original routing and backup number
- monitor forwarding health and provide a quick disable/rollback path
- document loop prevention; never transfer back to the forwarded main line
- reject activation unless `human_transfer_number != forwarded_main_number`, the transfer number differs from the AI inbound number, and the route does not indirectly return to the AI
- use number porting only as a fallback when conditional forwarding is unreliable and the restaurant explicitly accepts the migration risk

Pilot risk: carrier forwarding failures may be a larger support burden than voice-agent failures. Interview and test this workflow before committing to overflow as the default.

22. Compliance Requirements — Launch Blockers
The system should account for:
- consent requirements for call recording
- state-specific recording rules, including two-party-consent jurisdictions
- disclosure that the caller is speaking with an AI assistant
- TCPA considerations for automated texting/calling
- privacy policy
- terms of service
- deletion/export requests
- appropriate data retention
- secure access to recordings/transcripts
- opt-out handling for SMS
Recording is OFF by default. Enabling recording or full transcript retention requires an explicit location-level legal checklist and approved policy. Do not assume one national consent rule applies everywhere.
The opening disclosure and a human/non-recorded alternative must be defined before a pilot goes live.

Pricing should be tested, not assumed.

37. Biggest Risks
Risk 1 — Low event-call volume
Some restaurants may only receive a handful of event calls.
Mitigation:
Validate phone-originated event volume in 10–20 interviews before building telephony depth. Kill criterion: if the median target restaurant reports fewer than approximately 8 qualifying event calls per month, stop the phone-first product build and pivot toward web, email, and social intake. Do not rationalize a low-volume result based on outliers.

Risk 2 — Restaurants already have event forms
Mitigation:
The product captures the phone inquiries their existing event CRM misses and feeds the CRM by email, CSV, or webhook. The local leads table is a fallback, not the differentiator.

Risk 3 — AI makes incorrect statements
Mitigation:
Structured verified knowledge base + strict fallback behavior.

Risk 4 — Voice AI infrastructure cost
Mitigation:
Bottom-up cost model, limited included minutes, hard overage, overflow positioning, and measured call-duration/latency data.

Risk 5 — Customers expect full reservation automation
Mitigation:
Position product explicitly as a revenue/event desk initially.

Risk 6 — Generic AI receptionist competitors add the feature
Likely.
Defensibility must therefore accumulate through:
- reliable phone capture above every event CRM
- restaurant-specific workflows and verified knowledge
- measured recovered-revenue outcomes
- event-sales distribution partnerships
Not dashboard breadth or merely the voice agent.
