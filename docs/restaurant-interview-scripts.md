# Restaurant Interview Scripts

Use these scripts to validate how restaurants handle private dining, catering, and large-party inquiries. Keep the first conversation focused on their current workflow; don’t lead with a price or ask whether they would pay a hypothetical monthly amount.

## Brief founder introduction

Use the short version on calls so the conversation stays focused on the restaurant. The longer version can be used when someone asks about your background.

**Short version:** “I’ve spent my career in hospitality, from resort and restaurant operations to opening and managing restaurants in Los Angeles. I’m drawing on that experience as I research how restaurants handle event inquiries.”

**Longer version:** “I began my hospitality career at Grand Wailea Resort & Spa. My first restaurant job was at Wolfgang Puck’s Spago, and I later worked at Nick’s Fishmarket on Maui. After moving to Los Angeles, I joined the opening team at Waterloo and City with Carlos Tomazos (Per Se and Gilt) and Chef Brendan Collins (Melisse). I then helped open Larry’s in Venice and became its General Manager. Belcampo Meat Company recruited me as General Manager to open its Santa Monica flagship; I also assisted with its Downtown Los Angeles and San Francisco openings. I’ve also worked in fast-casual operations at Golden Road Brewing and Mendocino Farms.”

## Phone interview

### Opening

“Hi, is this the person who handles private dining, catering, or event inquiries? I’m [name]. I’m researching how independent restaurants handle those inquiries, especially calls that come in during service. Could I ask you a few questions? It should take about 10 minutes.”

If useful, follow with the short founder introduction above, then move to the questions.

If they’re busy:

“No problem. Is there a better time, or is there someone else who handles event inquiries?”

### Questions

1. “About how many private dining, catering, or large-party inquiries do you get in a typical month?”
2. “How do most of those inquiries reach you—phone, website form, email, or social?”
3. “Thinking about the last busy week, what happens when someone calls and nobody can pick up?”
4. “Do you know roughly how many event-related calls go unanswered or reach voicemail in a month?”
5. “Who follows up, and how long does it usually take?”
6. “How do you track inquiries now? Do you use Tripleseat, Perfect Venue, or something else?”
7. “If an inquiry came in by phone, what information would your team need to follow up?”
8. “How is your phone service set up? Do you know whether unanswered calls can forward to another number?”
9. “What would make you uncomfortable about an automated assistant answering an unanswered call?”

### Close

“Thanks, this is really helpful. I’m checking whether phone is an important enough channel to build around. Would you be open to a follow-up conversation if I put together a small pilot concept?”

If they report strong phone volume and clear pain, ask:

“Would you consider being one of a few restaurants to evaluate a pilot once the workflow is defined? No commitment today.”

## Outreach email

**Subject:** Quick question about private dining and event inquiries

Hi [Name],

I’m researching how independent restaurants handle private dining, catering, and large-party inquiries—especially calls that arrive when the team is busy.

I’ve spent my career in hospitality, including restaurant openings and general management, and am drawing on that experience for this research.

Could I ask you a few questions in a 10–15 minute call? I’m trying to understand how many inquiries come by phone, what happens when a call is missed, and how your team tracks and follows up on leads.

This is research for a product I’m considering; I’m not assuming phone is the right channel or that you need another CRM.

Would [time option 1] or [time option 2] work?
