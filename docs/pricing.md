# EstomoAI pricing and sales guide

## Positioning

Do not lead with “AI.” Business owners have heard enough magical robot nonsense. Lead with the problem:

- unanswered calls
- interrupted employees
- lost revenue

## Core restaurant sales pitch

When your restaurant gets busy, the telephone doesn’t stop—but your employees have guests standing in front of them.

EstomoAI installs a managed AI receptionist that answers missed, overflow, and after-hours calls using information your restaurant approves. It can:

- answer common questions
- collect reservation requests
- capture catering and private-event leads
- take messages
- transfer calls that need a person

It does not replace your staff, confirm anything without authorization, or guess when it does not know. We configure it, test it, monitor the calls, and improve it based on what customers actually ask.

The goal is simple: answer more customers without pulling employees away from the guests already inside your restaurant.

## General small-business version

How many calls reach your business when your employees are already helping someone, working in the field, or unavailable after hours?

EstomoAI installs a managed AI receptionist that answers those calls, handles approved questions, captures qualified requests, and transfers important calls to your team.

We build it around your business rules, connect it to the systems you already use, test it before launch, and monitor it afterward. It does not replace your employees—it protects their time and prevents valuable callers from disappearing into voicemail or calling a competitor.

## 30-second pitch

We help small businesses stop losing customers when nobody can answer the phone. EstomoAI installs a managed AI receptionist that answers routine questions, captures qualified leads, and routes important calls to the right person. Your team controls what it can say and do, and we manage the setup, testing, and ongoing improvement.

## Sales-conversation structure

Do not immediately demonstrate features. Ask questions first:

1. Approximately how many calls do you receive each week?
2. When are calls hardest to answer?
3. What are people usually calling about?
4. What happens when nobody answers?
5. Which calls are most valuable?
6. How much employee time is spent repeating basic information?
7. Do you receive valuable calls after hours?
8. How many catering, reservation, estimate, or appointment opportunities are missed?
9. What would the system absolutely not be allowed to do?
10. If we recovered five additional qualified calls monthly, what would that be worth?

Then say:

> Based on what you told me, I would not automate everything. I would begin with the calls you are currently missing and the repetitive questions consuming staff time.

> We would establish your current call baseline, configure the receptionist using information you approve, test the important scenarios, and start with overflow or after-hours calls. You keep your existing number and your employees remain available for anything requiring human judgment.

> After the pilot, we compare the actual results with your baseline. If it isn’t creating measurable value, we don’t pretend otherwise.

## Missed-call cost formula

Use gross profit, not just sales revenue. Revenue can make the calculation sound inflated.

### General formula

```text
Monthly missed-call opportunity = C × M × Q × K × G
```

Where:

- `C` = inbound calls per month
- `M` = percentage missed or abandoned
- `Q` = percentage of missed calls that are qualified opportunities
- `K` = percentage of qualified opportunities that normally convert
- `G` = average gross profit from one converted caller

### Example: service business

```text
300 monthly calls
× 20% missed
× 50% qualified opportunities
× 35% normal conversion rate
× $250 average gross profit per completed job
= $2,625 estimated monthly missed-call opportunity
```

This is not guaranteed recovered revenue. It is an estimate of the value potentially exposed by missed calls.

### Restaurant formula

Restaurants have several call types, so calculate them separately:

```text
Total opportunity = Reservation opportunity + Takeout opportunity + Catering opportunity
```

#### Reservation opportunity

```text
Rm × Cr × P × A × Gm
```

- `Rm` = missed reservation calls
- `Cr` = expected conversion rate
- `P` = average party size
- `A` = average check per guest
- `Gm` = gross-margin percentage

#### Takeout opportunity

```text
Tm × Ct × O × Gm
```

- `Tm` = missed takeout calls
- `Ct` = expected conversion rate
- `O` = average takeout order
- `Gm` = gross-margin percentage

#### Catering opportunity

```text
Lm × Cc × Gc
```

- `Lm` = missed catering leads
- `Cc` = normal catering close rate
- `Gc` = average gross profit from a catering booking

Catering and private events may justify the system even when ordinary restaurant calls have modest value.

## Labor-interruption savings

The receptionist also protects employee time:

```text
Monthly interruption cost = Cr × D × W
```

- `Cr` = repetitive calls per month
- `D` = average call duration in hours
- `W` = fully loaded hourly employee cost

Example:

```text
500 repetitive calls
× 0.05 hours per call
× $24 fully loaded hourly labor cost
= $600 estimated monthly interruption cost
```

This does not mean an employee can be eliminated. It means approximately $600 of labor time is being redirected into repetitive telephone work.

## Break-even and ROI

### Break-even formula

```text
Calls needed to break even = Monthly service price / Average gross profit per recovered customer
```

If EstomoAI costs $599 monthly and one converted caller produces $150 in gross profit:

```text
$599 / $150 = 3.99
```

The system needs approximately four additional conversions per month to cover its monthly price.

### ROI formula

```text
ROI = ((Incremental gross profit − EstomoAI cost) / EstomoAI cost) × 100
```

Only call it incremental gross profit when the customer can reasonably attribute the outcome to the system.

## Objections and responses

### “My employees already answer the phone.”

That’s good—we aren’t trying to prevent them from doing that. We can begin with unanswered, overflow, and after-hours calls only. The question is what happens when everyone is busy or no one is available.

### “Our customers don’t want to talk to AI.”

Some won’t, so the caller can request a person. The receptionist is for calls that would otherwise ring unanswered, go to voicemail, or interrupt an employee during service. Identify it as an automated assistant instead of pretending it is human.

### “It will sound robotic.”

Judge it by a live demonstration, not a promise. Voice quality is only one part of the system. The more important test is whether it understands callers, gives approved answers, and transfers correctly.

### “What if it gives the wrong answer?”

Restrict it to information the business approves, define subjects it may not answer, and require it to transfer when uncertain. Test it before launch and review failures afterward. High-risk questions—such as allergens, safety incidents, or payment disputes—always go to a person.

### “I don’t want it confirming reservations.”

It won’t. The first version collects reservation requests and clearly says they are pending review. It can only confirm a booking after an authorized employee or connected reservation system returns a real confirmation.

### “We already have voicemail.”

Voicemail records a problem for someone to handle later. The receptionist can answer common questions immediately, collect structured information, identify urgency, and route important calls. Compare both approaches during the pilot.

### “It’s too expensive.”

Calculate it from actual call volume, missed-call rate, and customer value. If the potential value is not meaningfully above the cost, do not recommend installing it.

### “I don’t want to replace my staff.”

Neither do we. The system handles overflow and repetitive calls while employees focus on work requiring judgment, hospitality, and accountability.

### “What happens if the system goes down?”

Configure a fallback route that returns calls to the normal telephone flow, backup number, or voicemail. The customer also needs a defined method for disabling the AI without waiting for us.

### “Will I lose my existing telephone number?”

No. The pilot can use unanswered-call forwarding, so the business retains its existing number. Document number ownership and cancellation procedures before launch.

### “I’m worried about customer privacy.”

Collect only the information required for the request, isolate each business’s data, and define recording, transcription, access, retention, and deletion settings before launch.

### “Can I cancel?”

Yes. The agreement defines how routing is removed, data is exported or deleted, and any EstomoAI-managed telephone number is transferred when applicable.

### “I tried an AI bot before and it was terrible.”

That is exactly why this is a managed implementation rather than a generic bot subscription. Design it around the customer’s specific calls, test failure cases, and improve it using observed results.
